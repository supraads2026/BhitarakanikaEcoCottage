Into theMangroves

Creeks, crocodiles, kingfishers and the slow rhythm of the tide — here's what a day in Bhitarkanika can hold.

01

Mangrove Boat Safari

Best timeOct – Mar
Duration2.5 – 5 hrs
WhereFrom 5 ghats

The heart of every visit. A motorboat carries you along wide rivers and into narrow creeks lined with mangrove roots — the only way to truly see Bhitarkanika. Watch for crocodiles on the mudbanks, monitor lizards, spotted deer and kingfishers flashing blue across the water.

02

Saltwater Crocodile Spotting

Best timeNov – Feb, sunny mid-mornings
DurationOn the safari
WhereCreeks & mudbanks

Bhitarkanika is home to about 70% of India's estuarine crocodiles — 1,858 were counted in January 2026, including rare white ones. On sunny winter days they haul out on the banks to bask. Keep your camera ready and your hands inside the boat!

03

Bird Watching

Best timeNov – Feb
DurationHalf day
WhereCreeks & Bagagahana

Over 200 species have been recorded here. Look for eight kinds of kingfisher, herons, egrets, storks and, in winter, flocks of migratory birds. At Bagagahana near Surajpore creek, thousands of water birds nest together in a spectacular heronry.

04

Dangmal & Bhitarkanika

Best timeOct – Apr
DurationFull day
WhereInside the park

Dangmal is the park's main island hub, with the crocodile research & breeding centre, a deer park and nature trails. Nearby Bhitarkanika forest block has a watchtower over the canopy and an old temple in the forest.

05

Gahirmatha Olive Ridley Turtles

Best timeUsually Feb – Mar
DurationSeasonal
WhereGahirmatha coast

Gahirmatha beach is one of the world's largest nesting sites for Olive Ridley sea turtles. In mass-nesting years, lakhs of turtles come ashore over a few nights. Dates change every year and access is restricted — ask us for the latest.

06

Village Life & Odia Food

Best timeAll year
Duration1 – 2 hrs
WhereAround Kasturikona

Walk through nearby villages, see fishing boats come in, and sit down to a proper Odia meal — fresh river fish and prawns, dalma, saga, and pakhala on hot days. Simple, local and delicious.

Wildlife you may see

Who lives in the mangroves

🐊

Saltwater crocodile

The largest living reptile — some here grow over 6 metres.

🦌

Spotted deer

Often seen grazing at the forest edge near Dangmal.

🦎

Water monitor

Large lizards sunning on banks and swimming the creeks.

🐦

Kingfishers

Eight species, including the striking black-capped kingfisher.

🐗

Wild boar

Found in the forest blocks, usually near dusk.

🐬

Dolphins

Irrawaddy and other dolphins are sometimes seen near the river mouths.
