BhitarkanikaNational Park

India's second-largest mangrove forest, a Ramsar wetland, and the kingdom of the saltwater crocodile.

About the park

Where rivers meet the sea

In Kendrapara district of Odisha, the Brahmani, Baitarani and Dhamra rivers spread into a vast delta of tidal creeks and mangrove islands before meeting the Bay of Bengal.

Bhitarkanika is India's second-largest mangrove ecosystem and a Ramsar wetland of international importance. It protects the country's largest population of saltwater crocodiles, rich birdlife, and — on its coast at Gahirmatha — one of the world's great sea-turtle nesting beaches. About one lakh visitors come every year.

Rules

Park rules & permits

  • Entry permits are required; fees are set by the Forest Department and may change
  • Camera and guide fees are charged separately
  • Carry a government photo ID for every adult
  • Plastic is completely banned inside the park
  • Stay on boats and marked trails; do not disturb wildlife
  • Park hours are roughly daytime only — confirm timings with us

Inside the park

Places to see

  • Dangmal — crocodile research & breeding centre, deer park
  • Bhitarkanika forest block — watchtower and old forest temple
  • Khola & Gupti — main entry gates
  • Bagagahana — heronry near Surajpore creek
  • Gahirmatha — Olive Ridley turtle nesting coast
  • Habalikhati — beach and nature camp
